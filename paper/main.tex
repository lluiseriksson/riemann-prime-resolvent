\begin{abstract}
Recent work constructs self-adjoint spectral approximants from Euler products
over primes up to $\lambda^2$ and reports striking numerical agreement with low
non-trivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function.  A rigorous convergence theorem
would imply the Riemann hypothesis.  We formulate a research programme based on
the positive observable
\[
\frac12\Tr(D^2+xI)^{-1},
\]
rather than direct convergence of regularized determinants.  The central
abstract observation is that a holomorphic extension to the slit plane of the
critical-line logarithmic-derivative target excludes non-real zeros of $\Xi$.
Positive Stieltjes approximants with one-point control provide such extensions
through normal-family compactness.  On the arithmetic side, the target admits
an absolutely convergent prime expansion for $x>1/4$, with an elementary
explicit tail majorant.  We separate the desired comparison into spectral,
model, and prime errors, describe a certificate-driven finite-dimensional
route, and give a Lean~4 formalization plan.  The accompanying repository
contains an axiom-free elementary Lean seed and a strict publication gate.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Let
\[
\xi(s)=\frac12s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s),
\qquad
\Xi(z)=\xi\!\left(\frac12+iz\right).
\]
The Riemann hypothesis is equivalent to all zeros of the entire even function
$\Xi$ being real.  The Hilbert--Pólya philosophy seeks a self-adjoint operator
whose spectrum encodes those zeros.  Connes, Consani and Moscovici recently
proposed self-adjoint operators obtained from rank-one perturbations of a
scaling spectral triple on $[\lambda^{-1},\lambda]$; their construction uses
Euler products over primes $p\leq \lambda^2$, and numerical experiments show
high-precision agreement with low Riemann zeros.  They state explicitly that a
rigorous proof of convergence would establish RH and discuss regularized
determinants as approximants to $\Xi$~\cite{CCM2025}.

A companion theorem of Connes and van Suijlekom shows, under lower-bounded
self-adjointness and a simple isolated lowest eigenvalue with an even
eigenfunction, that the Fourier transform of that eigenfunction has only real
zeros~\cite{CvS2025}.  This supplies a real-zero mechanism for each finite or
semilocal model but does not prove convergence to $\Xi$.

The present programme proposes to compare squared resolvents instead of entire
determinants.  Resolvents are positive, suppress high spectral values, and are
stable under perturbation.  More importantly, the corresponding scalar
functions lie in the Stieltjes--Nevanlinna class, where local boundedness and
normal-family arguments are natural.

The contribution of this draft is organizational and reductive.  It gives a
complete proof of an abstract slit-plane extension criterion, isolates an
explicit arithmetic target and prime tail, and states precisely what remains to
be shown for the concrete operators.  It should be read as a blueprint for
formalization and further analysis, not as a completed RH argument.

\section{The critical-line resolvent target}

For $x>1/4$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:target}
\XiR(x)=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{x}}
\frac{\xi'}{\xi}\!\left(\frac12+\sqrt{x}\right).
\end{equation}
The restriction $x>1/4$ ensures that $\sigma=1/2+\sqrt{x}>1$, so the Euler
product is absolutely convergent and $\zeta(\sigma)\neq0$.

If RH holds and the positive zeros of $\Xi$ are $\gamma_k$, counted with
multiplicity, the symmetric canonical product formally gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stieltjes-rh}
\XiR(x)=\sum_{k\ge1}\frac1{\gamma_k^2+x}.
\end{equation}
Thus $\XiR$ is a Stieltjes transform of a positive discrete measure.  A final
paper must verify the canonical-product normalization carefully.  The
one-way criterion below does not assume~\eqref{eq:stieltjes-rh}.

\section{A slit-plane extension criterion}

Let
\[
\Omega=\C\setminus(-\infty,0].
\]

\begin{theorem}[Slit-plane extension criterion]\label{thm:slit}
Suppose there is a holomorphic function $S:\Omega\to\C$ and a nonempty open
interval $I\subset(1/4,\infty)$ such that
\[
S(x)=\XiR(x)\qquad(x\in I).
\]
Then all zeros of $\Xi$ are real.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For $z$ in the open upper half-plane define
\[
M(z)=2zS(-z^2).
\]
The map $z\mapsto-z^2$ takes the upper half-plane into $\Omega$.  Indeed, if
$\operatorname{Re}z=0$, then $-z^2>0$; otherwise
$\operatorname{Im}(-z^2)=-2\operatorname{Re}z\operatorname{Im}z\neq0$.
Hence $M$ is holomorphic.

Take $z=iy$ with $y>0$ and $y^2\in I$.  The functional equation
$\xi(s)=\xi(1-s)$ gives $\xi'(s)=-\xi'(1-s)$, and therefore
\[
-\frac{\Xi'(iy)}{\Xi(iy)}
=i\frac{\xi'}{\xi}\!\left(\frac12+y\right)
=2iyS(y^2)=M(iy).
\]
The function $-\Xi'/\Xi$ is holomorphic away from the discrete zeros of $\Xi$.
The upper half-plane minus a discrete subset is connected, so the identity
theorem yields
\[
M(z)=-\frac{\Xi'(z)}{\Xi(z)}
\]
away from the zeros.

If $z_0$ were an upper-half-plane zero of multiplicity $m>0$, local
factorization would give $\Xi(z)=(z-z_0)^m g(z)$ with $g(z_0)\neq0$.  Hence
\[
-\frac{\Xi'(z)}{\Xi(z)}=-\frac{m}{z-z_0}-\frac{g'(z)}{g(z)},
\]
which has a nonremovable pole at $z_0$, contradicting holomorphy of $M$.
Conjugation symmetry excludes zeros in the lower half-plane.  Thus every zero
is real.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The theorem only requires an extension on the slit plane agreeing with the
target on one interval.  It does not require prior identification of every
zero, global determinant convergence, or an explicit inverse spectral theorem.
\end{remark}

\section{Stieltjes approximants and compactness}

Let $\nu_j$ be positive measures on $[0,\infty)$ and define
\[
S_j(w)=\int_0^\infty\frac{d\nu_j(t)}{t+w},\qquad w\in\Omega.
\]
For a discrete paired spectrum $\{\pm\gamma_{j,k}\}$ one expects
\[
S_j(x)=\sum_k\frac1{\gamma_{j,k}^2+x}
=\frac12\Tr(D_j^2+xI)^{-1}.
\]

\begin{proposition}[One-point control]\label{prop:normal}
Fix $x_0>0$.  If $\sup_j S_j(x_0)<\infty$, then the family $\{S_j\}$ is locally
bounded on $\Omega$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For each compact $K\subset\Omega$, continuity and avoidance of the negative
axis imply the existence of $C_K$ with
\[
\frac1{|t+w|}\leq\frac{C_K}{t+x_0}
\]
for all $t\ge0$ and $w\in K$.  Integrating gives
$|S_j(w)|\le C_K S_j(x_0)$.
\end{proof}

Montel's theorem then gives compact-open convergent subsequences.  If $S_j(x)$
converges to $\XiR(x)$ on an open interval, any subsequential limit supplies the
extension required by Theorem~\ref{thm:slit}.

\begin{theorem}[Abstract prime--resolvent criterion]\label{thm:abstract}
Assume $S_j$ are Stieltjes transforms of positive measures, are uniformly
bounded at one positive point, and converge pointwise to $\XiR$ on a nonempty
open interval contained in $(1/4,\infty)$.  Then RH holds.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Combine Proposition~\ref{prop:normal}, Montel compactness, the identity theorem,
and Theorem~\ref{thm:slit}.
\end{proof}

\section{The arithmetic side}

For real $\sigma>1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:logxi}
\frac{\xi'}{\xi}(\sigma)
=\frac1\sigma+\frac1{\sigma-1}-\frac12\log\pi
+\frac12\psi(\sigma/2)
-\sum_{n\ge2}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{n^\sigma},
\end{equation}
where $\psi=\Gamma'/\Gamma$ and $\Lambda$ is the von Mangoldt function.
Putting $y=\sqrt{x}$ and $\sigma=1/2+y$, define the truncation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:PX}
P_X(x)=\frac1{2y}\left[
\frac1\sigma+\frac1{\sigma-1}-\frac12\log\pi
+\frac12\psi(\sigma/2)
-\sum_{2\le n\le X}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{n^\sigma}
\right].
\end{equation}

Fix $\delta>0$ and suppose $\sigma\ge1+\delta$.  Since
$0\le\Lambda(n)\le\log n$, a decreasing-integral comparison for $X\ge3$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tail}
\sum_{n>X}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{n^\sigma}
\le X^{-\delta}
\left(\frac{\log X}{\delta}+\frac1{\delta^2}\right).
\end{equation}
This produces a uniform explicit bound for
$|\XiR(x)-P_X(x)|$ on any compact interval corresponding to
$y\ge1/2+\delta$.

The Lean seed formalizes the closed-form majorant and its sign.  The full
sum--integral comparison and log-derivative formula are formalization targets.

\section{The three-part error budget}

Let $S_{\lambda,N}$ be a concrete spectral observable and
$\widetilde S_\lambda$ a source-audited model observable.  Then
\begin{align}
|S_{\lambda,N}-P_{\lambda^2}|
&\le |S_{\lambda,N}-\widetilde S_\lambda|
 +|\widetilde S_\lambda-\XiR|
 +|\XiR-P_{\lambda^2}|\\
&=:E_{\mathrm{spectral}}+E_{\mathrm{model}}+E_{\mathrm{prime}}.
\end{align}
This elementary inequality and addition of component limits are already
encoded in Lean.

The arithmetic term is controlled by~\eqref{eq:tail}.  The model term depends
on exact Fourier/Mellin conventions and is presently under source audit.  The
spectral term is the decisive open contribution.

\section{Spectral alignment and finite certificates}

Let $A$ be a finite Hermitian matrix with lowest eigenvalues
$\epsilon_0<\epsilon_1$ and gap $g=\epsilon_1-\epsilon_0$.  For a normalized
trial vector $v$ define
\[
r=\langle Av,v\rangle-\epsilon_0.
\]
Expansion in an eigenbasis yields
\[
\inf_\theta\|v-e^{i\theta}\xi_0\|\le\sqrt{2r/g}.
\]
A Galerkin projection tail $\tau$ then motivates the scalar defect
\[
\eta_{\lambda,N}
=\|k_\lambda\|\sqrt{2r_{\lambda,N}/g_{\lambda,N}}+2\tau_{\lambda,N}.
\]

For computation, exact knowledge of $\epsilon_0$ should be replaced by a
residual and a certified separation bound.  The external program exports
rational or interval data; Lean checks Hermitian symmetry, residual inequalities,
gap/separation conditions, and the resulting scalar bound.

No numerical result is accepted as proof without a certificate and a theorem
connecting the certificate to the infinite model.

\section{Candidate rate and its status}

An exploratory calculation suggested that a bound
\[
\eta_{\lambda,N(\lambda)}=O(\lambda^{-q}),\qquad q>1/2,
\]
may be enough to force total convergence on a suitable interval, with optimized
exponent
\[
\rho(q)=\min\left\{\frac{2q-1}{3},\frac23\right\}.
\]
The scalar positivity and saturation properties of this expression are included
in Lean.  However, the analytic derivation of the $2/3$ ceiling depends on a
candidate enlargement of a prolate/model estimate.  Before this can be stated
as a theorem, one must verify the precise transform normalization, support,
constants, and source hypotheses in~\cite{CCM2025}.  Accordingly this section is
a research target, not a claimed result.

\section{Lean formalization architecture}

Mathlib supplies definitions of the Riemann zeta function, a pole-removed
completed zeta, a functional equation, and a proposition named
`RiemannHypothesis`.  The formalization is divided into:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
\item the exact $\xi/\Xi$ bridge;
\item geometry of the slit plane and the map $z\mapsto-z^2$;
\item local analytic factorization and logarithmic-derivative poles;
\item the extension criterion;
\item the explicit prime tail;
\item finite Stieltjes and spectral certificates;
\item normal-family compactness;
\item the concrete unbounded operator.
\end{enumerate}

The seed repository contains no project axioms and represents open results as
propositions or explicit fields of a future package.  The operator layer will
not be introduced until the abstract analytic chain is complete.

\section{Publication criteria}

A first formalization paper becomes defensible after the abstract slit-plane
criterion, the Mathlib RH bridge, the prime-tail theorem, and the finite
certificate layer are all complete and reproducible.  A substantive analytic
paper additionally needs a new concrete estimate for the Galerkin, gap, or
state-alignment error.  A claim of RH requires full convergence, absence of
circular use of Weil positivity, and independent specialist review.

\section{Conclusion}

The squared-resolvent/Stieltjes viewpoint converts an imprecise aspiration
(``the spectral determinant converges to $\Xi$'') into several separable and
falsifiable tasks.  The extension criterion shows that convergence on one real
interval can be enough, while positivity provides compactness and the Euler
product provides an explicit arithmetic target.  The remaining hard step is
not hidden: it is a quantitative, non-circular comparison between the concrete
lowest spectral state and the target model, together with all trace and domain
properties needed to pass to the limit.
